Reciprocal academic–industry mentoring

I mentor industrial staff and collaborate through two-way mentoring: academic specialists share research methods, technology evaluation and analytical practice, while industry colleagues contribute operational expertise, constraints and implementation experience. The aim is mutual professional learning and stronger, more relevant projects.

How I approach learning

Theory meets practice. Students build and interrogate actual systems rather than relying on demonstrations alone.

Accessibility matters. Support for local open-source models and financially inclusive learning pathways, with appropriate alternatives.

Human judgement stays central. AI is a co-pilot, not an autopilot—learners practise challenging, validating and governing outputs.

Capability grows through mentoring. Seven GTAs mentored in teaching practice, including two who achieved AFHEA recognition.
